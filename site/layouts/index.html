The Water of Life

Contemporary practitioners are already generating observations through lived practice, but those experiences have rarely been collected in a structured, public, searchable form.

So this archive is beginning that record.

The Water of Life is a public, participatory archive of first-person accounts from people who engage with auto-urine practice — in their own words, in observational language, with the full picture of their lives alongside it. No claims. No diagnoses. No prescriptions.

Why this exists

Urine has a long history of medicinal and ritual use across multiple traditions. Auto-urine practice appears explicitly in yogic sources such as the Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā, while Ayurvedic texts document broader medicinal uses of human and animal urine. Armstrong's The Water of Life (1944) documented decades of Western case histories.

Published literature on the subject does exist — historical reviews, case reports, laboratory work, hypotheses. But as of August 2026, no controlled clinical trial evaluating auto-urine practice as a therapeutic intervention in humans has been identified. The absence of that evidence is not evidence that the practice works. It is not evidence that it does not. It is a gap.

And there is another layer of evidence that has gone largely uncollected: structured first-person accounts of what contemporary practitioners actually do, what else is happening in their lives, and what they observe over time. That is what this archive collects.

My name is Ana. I am a practitioner myself, and I started this project because I got tired of waiting for someone else to do it. Every entry is reviewed by hand. Every interview is a real conversation. This is not a data-harvesting exercise — it is a labour of care, and I take the responsibility of holding people's experiences seriously.

Contribute your experience

If you are living this practice, your experience belongs in the record. Not because it proves anything — but because it exists, and right now, no one is counting it.

Every entry that goes into this archive is something that did not exist before. Every story shared is one more piece of a record that is long overdue.

  • Fill out the template — a structured set of questions you can answer in writing
  • Book a conversation — talk through your experience with the archive steward, or just ask questions before deciding

You choose how you are identified: real name, pseudonym, or fully anonymous. Your experience. Your terms.
