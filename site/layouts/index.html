The Water of Life

People are already living this practice. Every day, they notice things — sometimes remarkable, sometimes ordinary, sometimes difficult, sometimes nothing at all. Most of those observations disappear.

The Water of Life is a place to leave them.

What did you do? What did you notice? What else was happening? That is the project. Not proving anything. Just stopping observations from disappearing.

Why this exists

Auto-urine practice has a long history across multiple traditions, but accounts of what contemporary practitioners actually do and observe have gone largely uncollected. The history and evidence landscape are documented more fully in the founding document. This archive has a simpler purpose: to preserve the observations that otherwise disappear.

My name is Ana. I am a practitioner myself, and I come from a family of record-keepers. My great-aunt kept journals everywhere she lived — from the coffee fincas of southern Mexico to the Arizona desert — and those journals are now primary historical sources in libraries and universities. This archive carries the same impulse: observations recorded carefully, by hand, so they last.

The Water of Life is a cooperative archive, maintained by Ana Seahawk with the support of Fiona Gardner and Megan Macdonald. Every account is read personally and held with care.

Share an account

What did you do? What did you notice? What else was changing? That is all an observation needs to be. Two minutes. A few sentences.

You can come back and add more over time. This is a field notebook, not a one-time survey.

  • Share an account — a short, structured form. Takes two to three minutes.
  • Book a conversation — for experiences that need depth, or if you just want to talk before contributing

Each account is retold for the archive with light editing. A name appears only when one is given: real name, pseudonym, or none.
